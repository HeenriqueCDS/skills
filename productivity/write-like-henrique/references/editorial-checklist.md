
# Format branches

## Technical article

Roughly 900–1,600 words unless requested otherwise.

Lead with the engineering dilemma and the thesis; one paragraph of context; concrete alternatives; a working or clearly illustrative code/architecture example when useful; decision criteria and failure modes; ending that answers the opening question.

Treat versions, benchmarks, costs, and APIs as checkable claims. Explain why a choice was made before listing implementation details. Use a minimal number of headings.

## LinkedIn technical post

Roughly 120–250 words.

Open with a non-clickbait observation or earned question. One example or miniature story, one meaningful tradeoff, one takeaway.

Use tight paragraphs that read naturally on mobile. Prefer no hashtags; use at most two if the user wants them. No forced "What do you think?" engagement bait.

## Thread or short social post

One idea per post. First post explains the useful tension. Later posts deliver examples and counterarguments. Finish with the actionable insight, not a sales pitch. Respect a requested character limit.

## Build in public

Identify the actual feature or experiment, the decision being tested, what worked or failed (only if supplied), and what's next.

Be open about limitations and changes of mind; avoid manufacturing traction, launch dates, or customer testimonials.

## Architecture decision breakdown

Problem and constraints → viable options → tradeoffs by operations, complexity, performance, cost and team experience → contextual choice → reconsideration trigger.

If the user has not made the final choice, show alternatives neutrally rather than inventing their decision.
